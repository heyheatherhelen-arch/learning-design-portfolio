01 / Orientation · 3 minutes

What should students still know after today's AI tools are gone?

You are preparing to teach in a fictional AI and Data Analytics Advanced Technical Certificate (ATC). Your design challenge: turn durable AI competencies into observable learning and assessment evidence without mistaking tool proficiency for informed agency.

Your performance task: Use CAP3744 as a worked example, investigate an imperfect AI dashboard, critique assessment options, diagnose and revise an ABCDE outcome, align evidence to scoring, and identify a program-level transfer opportunity.

By the end, you will be able to

  1. Distinguish tool-centered, workforce-centered, and student-centered AI outcomes.
  2. Use UNESCO competency dimensions and the ABCDE Employability Model to specify observable performance.
  3. Evaluate whether an assessment provides evidence of capability, judgment, and agency.
  4. Draft a revised outcome and assessment you can bring to the facilitated workshop.

Context: The ATC and course title are publicly described educational contexts. All faculty requests, scenarios, data, outcomes, and assessments here are fictional demonstrations, not approved institutional curriculum.

02 / Distinguish · 4 minutes

Three orientations to AI education

In the Breda presentation, Mullins and Yousef distinguish three questions. Each matters, but they are not interchangeable.

Tool-centered

Student as user

Can you operate it?

Evidence: tool proficiency

Workforce-centered

Student as worker

Can you create value with it?

Evidence: productivity and output

Student-centered

Student as agent

Can you determine its value?

Evidence: informed choice about whether, why, when, and for whom to use AI

Inspect the data-quality audit
  • Approximately 15% of maintenance records are missing facility identifiers.
  • Component codes have inconsistent formatting.

Decision lab · Several responses may be defensible

Choose a response you could justify to a maintenance director. Your reasoning matters more than selecting one prescribed action.

Pause: Request audited exposure data and a coding review before authorizing replacement.

Pilot: Permit limited AI-assisted monitoring with human review, predefined stopping criteria, and no automatic replacement.

Reject: Decline this recommendation because the current evidence does not support a fleet-wide decision; commission a transparent independent analysis.

What would make any option defensible?

Evidence should include exposure-adjusted rates (A 1.2, B 3.2, C 2.1 per 1,000 recorded hours), the limits of incomplete data, potential costs and safety impacts, and explicit safeguards or alternatives. No single response is automatically correct.

Faculty design lens: What assignment would require a student to discover these limitations independently, explain the decision, and show agency? Use that question in the outcome builder.

3 · Evidence-alignment matrix

Design one defensible competency-to-evidence alignment

Choose one competency and specify the student task, observable evidence, performance standard, and evaluation method. You will extend this alignment to the other two competencies with colleagues during the facilitated workshop.

Select the competency most important to your proposed CAP3744 assessment. All three matter; today you will fully align one and prepare to collaborate on the others.

In the 60-minute workshop: Share this completed alignment with a peer. Together, add alignments for the two remaining competencies, compare the evidence across all three, and revise any gaps or overlaps.
Annotated proficient exemplar — one row

Competency: Critical judgment. Task: Compare raw and exposure-adjusted component failures and audit missingness. Evidence: Reproducible calculations, a limitation log, and an annotated recommendation. Standard: Correct denominators and rates; at least two material data limitations explained; recommendation proportional to evidence. Evaluation: Instructor checks calculations against an answer key and scores the limitation log and justification using an analytic rubric. Why it works: The evidence can be inspected independently and the standard describes quality rather than mere completion.

Flawed exemplar — identify the alignment failure

Competency: Student agency. Task: Generate a maintenance memo using AI. Evidence: Screenshot of AI output. Standard: Submitted on time. Evaluation: Completion credit. Diagnosis: Nothing demonstrates that the student independently weighed alternatives, identified when AI should not be used, or justified consequences. A submission deadline is not a competency standard.

01 / The performance challenge

Teaching AI use is not the same as teaching AI judgment.

Faculty preparing for a new AI and Data Analytics Advanced Technical Certificate need a way to translate broad workforce competencies into measurable learning outcomes and assessments that remain useful as AI tools change.

Rather than ask whether students can operate a platform, this experience asks whether they can validate its output, identify consequences, and decide when an AI recommendation should be accepted, revised, or rejected.

Design brief

Audience
Engineering and computing faculty

Performance goal
Produce a revised course outcome and evidence-aligned assessment that can be evaluated against explicit criteria.

Learning environment
Self-paced preparation followed by a collaborative curriculum design studio.

Worked example
CAP3744 Data Analytics and Visualization, situated in a fictionalized ATC faculty onboarding context.

03 / Design rationale

Designed for transfer, not just completion.

Why these interactions?

Authentic problem: A plausible but flawed AI recommendation makes data validation and professional consequences consequential rather than abstract.

Productive difficulty: Faculty critique assessment options and a flawed outcome before composing their own response.

Progressive disclosure: The Apply section breaks a complex design brief into five focused stages.

Blended division of labor: Faculty independently align one competency; the facilitated studio extends the work to the remaining competencies through peer critique.

How performance is evaluated

The final artifact includes a revised outcome, an assessment brief, evidence standards, and a program-level transfer rationale. A five-criterion rubric supports review of technical capability, critical judgment, agency, ABCDE alignment, and evidence quality.

Feedback boundary: Automated feedback checks selections and completeness; it does not claim to judge the substantive quality of a faculty member’s design. That requires human review.

Evaluation plan: Compare draft quality before and after the studio and follow up on implementation. No pilot results or learning gains are claimed.

The goal is not simply to make students more capable users of AI, but to develop their capacity to determine when, why, and for whom AI is useful.
